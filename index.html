Jatin Arutla

I am a data scientist and independent researcher working on machine learning for neural data. I completed an MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning with Distinction at the University of Birmingham.

More recently, I have been studying the reliability of EEG decoders and pretrained encoders when the reference or montage changes. I have also worked on layer-resolved encoding models comparing speech and text model representations against ECoG responses. My broader interests are robust neural decoding, neural foundation models and representation learning for neural signals.

Research

Reference Shift in EEG Decoders and Pretrained Encoders

Independent research, ongoing

I treat a change in EEG reference as a controlled train-test shift and evaluate transfer across seven reference operators using CSP-LDA, ShallowConvNet, REVE and MIRepNet. Common average referencing removes much of the mismatch between global reference schemes, while Euclidean Alignment reduces the broader transfer gap to around 2-4 percentage points. I am also testing held-out reference families and adaptation with limited unlabeled target data.

Layer-Resolved Speech and Text Model Encoding of ECoG Responses

Independent research

I rebuilt a layer-resolved encoding analysis on the Podcast ECoG dataset using banded ridge regression and variance partitioning, then extended it with Qwen2.5 transcript representations. Later Whisper layers predicted neural responses better and contained more unique variance not explained by acoustic, phonetic and syntactic features.

Weakly Supervised Lung Disease Localization with Self-Supervised Pretraining

MSc thesis, University of Birmingham

I developed a mask-guided weakly supervised localisation framework on NIH ChestX-ray14. The method used LayerCAM disease masks across network layers and a shallow-feature perceptual loss for image alignment, improving mean IoU by 8.2% over the baseline.
